# Professional Writing Skill

## Core Principles
1. **Purpose first.** Before writing, finish this sentence: "After reading this, the reader will ______."
2. **Reader first.** Write for their knowledge, time, priorities, and likely reaction - not for yourself.
3. **Lead with the point (BLUF - bottom line up front).** Conclusion, decision, or ask in the first lines.
4. **Clarity over cleverness.** Plain words, short sentences, concrete specifics.
5. **Concise, not curt.** Cut words, not meaning. Every sentence must earn its place.
6. **Specific and evidence-based.** Numbers, names, dates, examples beat adjectives.
7. **Make action easy.** State who must do what by when.
8. **Edit ruthlessly.** First drafts are for thinking; editing is where quality happens.

## Pre-Writing Checklist
- **Audience:** who are they, what do they already know, what do they care about, what objections might they have?
- **Purpose:** inform / request / persuade / decide / document / apologize?
- **Key message:** one sentence.
- **Required action & deadline.**
- **Tone & formality:** match culture, relationship, and stakes.
- **Format & length:** match convention and reader's time.
- **Evidence needed:** facts, figures, quotes - verified.

## Structure Patterns
- **Inverted pyramid:** most important first, details after (news, updates, executive writing).
- **PREP:** Point -> Reason -> Example -> Point (persuasion, short arguments).
- **Problem -> Solution -> Benefit** (proposals).
- **Situation -> Complication -> Resolution** (business cases).
- **Chronological** for incident reports and project histories; **comparative** for options analysis.
- Use headings, short paragraphs (3-5 lines), one idea per paragraph, topic sentences, transitions, and lists for parallel items.

## Style Rules
**Words**
- Prefer simple words: use/help/show/start (not utilize/facilitate/demonstrate/commence).
- Strong verbs over nominalizations: "decide," not "make a decision."
- Active voice by default ("The team completed the audit"); passive only when the actor is unknown or unimportant.
- Cut filler: "in order to," "it should be noted that," "very," "really," "basically," "at this point in time."
- Define jargon and acronyms on first use or avoid them.
- Be precise with numbers, dates, units; avoid vague "soon," "significant," "many."
**Sentences**
- Average 15-20 words; vary rhythm; one main idea each.
- Put the subject and verb early; avoid burying the point in subordinate clauses.
- Parallel structure in lists and comparisons.
**Tone**
- Respectful, direct, confident but not arrogant; positive framing ("Here's what we can do") over blame.
- Avoid sarcasm, hype, excessive exclamation marks, and emotional language in conflict.
- Use inclusive, bias-free language; avoid idioms that don't translate for global readers.

## Document Templates

### Email
- **Subject:** specific and actionable ("Decision needed by Fri: vendor selection for Q1").
- **Opening:** the ask or key info in line 1-2.
- **Body:** brief context, details in short paragraphs or bullets.
- **Close:** explicit next step, owner, deadline; courteous sign-off.
- Length: ideally < 150 words; one topic per email; reply promptly and keep thread context clear.

### Memo
`TO / FROM / DATE / SUBJECT` -> Purpose & recommendation -> Background (brief) -> Analysis/options -> Recommendation & next steps.

### Executive Summary
Standalone, 1 page or less: problem/opportunity, key findings, recommendation, impact (numbers), cost/risk, decision/next step. Written last, read first.

### Report
Title -> Executive summary -> Introduction/purpose -> Method (if relevant) -> Findings (headed sections, visuals) -> Analysis/discussion -> Conclusions -> Recommendations -> Appendices/sources.

### Proposal
Client need -> Proposed solution -> Scope & deliverables -> Approach/timeline -> Team & credentials -> Pricing/ROI -> Risks & assumptions -> Terms & next steps.

### Cover Letter
Tailored to the role: hook (why this role/company) -> 2-3 achievements with measurable results mapped to requirements -> motivation/fit -> call to action. One page; no generic template language.

### Resume / CV
Reverse chronological, achievement bullets (verb + what + metric/impact), tailored keywords, consistent formatting, no filler, 1-2 pages.

### Press Release
Headline -> dateline & lead (who/what/when/where/why) -> supporting details and quote -> boilerplate -> media contact. Factual, no hype.

### Meeting Minutes / Status Update
Date, attendees, decisions, action items (owner + due date), risks/blockers, next meeting. Scannable.

### Difficult Messages (bad news, refusal, apology, feedback)
State the decision/issue directly and early, briefly explain the reason, acknowledge impact, offer alternatives or next steps, take accountability where appropriate, keep tone respectful. Don't bury the news; don't over-apologize.

### Persuasive Writing
Open with the reader's problem or benefit; present claim -> evidence -> reasoning; address the strongest objection fairly; close with a clear, easy call to action.

## Editing Process (multiple passes)
1. **Big picture:** Does it achieve the purpose? Is the main point first? Logical order? Anything missing or unnecessary?
2. **Structure:** headings, paragraphing, flow, transitions; move or cut sections.
3. **Clarity & concision:** shorten sentences, remove filler, replace jargon, convert passive to active, prefer concrete over abstract.
4. **Tone & audience fit:** read as the recipient. Any ambiguity, defensiveness, or unintended edge?
5. **Accuracy:** verify every fact, number, name, date, and quote.
6. **Proofread:** grammar, spelling, punctuation, formatting consistency, correct names and titles, links, attachments. Read aloud or reverse-read; change the font/view to catch errors.
7. **Final test:** can someone skim the headings/first sentences and understand the message?

## Common Problems & Fixes
| Problem | Fix |
|---|---|
| Burying the point | Move the conclusion/ask to the top |
| Long, tangled sentences | Split; one idea each |
| Vague claims | Add numbers, examples, names |
| Jargon/acronyms | Define or replace |
| Passive, abstract prose | Active voice, concrete subjects |
| Wall of text | Headings, short paragraphs, bullets |
| Unclear action | State who/what/when explicitly |
| Over-hedging ("I think maybe...") | Commit where evidence supports it |
| Defensive or harsh tone | Reframe around facts and solutions |
| Inconsistent formatting | Apply a style standard throughout |

## Adapting Tone (examples)
- Too stiff: "Please be advised that the meeting has been rescheduled." -> "The meeting has moved to Thursday at 2 pm."
- Too vague: "We saw good growth." -> "Revenue grew 18% year over year, to $4.2M."
- Too blunt: "Your report is wrong." -> "I found a discrepancy in the Q3 totals; here's what I see, and let's reconcile."

## Cross-Cultural & Accessibility Notes
- Use clear, literal language for international readers; avoid slang, sports metaphors, and idioms.
- Write dates unambiguously (e.g., 5 Nov 2026), specify time zones.
- Use descriptive link text, headings, and alt text; avoid conveying meaning by color alone.

## Ethics & Integrity
- Be truthful; don't overstate or mislead with selective facts.
- Cite sources; never plagiarize; disclose conflicts.
- Protect confidential information; consider who may read forwarded messages.

## Final Checklist
- [ ] Purpose and desired action clear in the first lines
- [ ] Written for the actual reader; right tone and formality
- [ ] Logical structure with scannable headings/paragraphs
- [ ] Concise, active, concrete; jargon defined
- [ ] Facts, numbers, names verified
- [ ] Next steps with owner and deadline
- [ ] Proofread (grammar, spelling, formatting, links/attachments)
- [ ] Would I be comfortable if this were forwarded to anyone?
