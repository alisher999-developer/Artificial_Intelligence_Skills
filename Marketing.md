# Marketing Skill

## Core Principles
1. **Customer first.** Start with the audience's problem, language, and motivations - not your product's features.
2. **Positioning before promotion.** Be clear who you serve, what you offer, and why you're the better choice.
3. **One message, one action.** Every asset has one core idea and one primary call to action.
4. **Right message, right person, right moment, right channel.**
5. **Test, measure, learn.** Treat campaigns as experiments; decisions follow data, not opinions.
6. **Honest marketing.** Claims must be truthful and substantiated; follow advertising, privacy, and platform rules. Never fabricate testimonials, reviews, or endorsements.
7. **Brand is cumulative; performance is immediate.** Balance long-term brand building with short-term conversion.
8. **Think full-funnel.** Awareness -> Consideration -> Conversion -> Retention -> Advocacy.

## Step 1 - Foundations

### Audience Research
- Define segments by needs and behaviors, not only demographics.
- **Jobs-to-be-done:** what progress is the customer trying to make? What triggers a search? What are the alternatives (including doing nothing)?
- Sources: customer interviews, surveys, sales and support transcripts, reviews, forums, social listening, analytics, competitor reviews.
- Build **personas/ICPs** grounded in evidence: goals, pains, objections, buying process, decision-makers, channels, vocabulary.
- Map the **customer journey** and key moments of truth.

### Competitive & Market Context
- Competitors' positioning, messaging, pricing, channels, content, strengths and gaps; where can you own distinct space?

### Positioning & Value Proposition
- Positioning statement: *For [target] who [need], [brand] is the [category] that [key benefit], because [reason to believe], unlike [alternatives].*
- Differentiators must be **meaningful to buyers, provable, and defensible**.
- Build a **messaging house**: core promise -> 3 pillars -> proof points (data, testimonials, case studies, awards, guarantees).
- Define brand voice (3-4 adjectives, do/don't examples) and keep it consistent.

## Step 2 - Strategy & Planning
- **SMART objectives** tied to business goals (pipeline, revenue, signups, retention) - not vanity metrics.
- Marketing plan: objective, audience, offer, message, channels, budget, timeline, owners, KPIs, risks.
- **Channel selection:** choose where your audience is and where you can win; fewer channels done well beat many done poorly.
- **Budget:** allocate by expected return; keep a test budget (10-20%); model CAC, LTV, payback.
- **Offer:** what's the compelling reason to act now? (value, risk reversal, incentive, urgency that is truthful).
- 4Ps/7Ps checkup: Product, Price, Place, Promotion (+ People, Process, Physical evidence).

## Step 3 - Channels & Tactics

### Content Marketing
- Align content with funnel stage and search/social intent: educational, comparison, proof, product, community.
- Blog, guides, ebooks, webinars, podcasts, video, tools/calculators, case studies, original research.
- Content engine: pillar content -> repurpose into posts, emails, clips, graphics -> distribute -> refresh.
- Headline and hook quality determine reach; test several.

### SEO (see SEO skill)
Capture existing demand through organic search with intent-matched content and technical health.

### Email & Lifecycle
- Build your own list with a clear value exchange (lead magnet, newsletter, trial).
- Sequences: welcome/onboarding, nurture, abandoned cart, post-purchase, re-engagement, win-back, referral.
- Segment by behavior and lifecycle stage; personalize beyond first name.
- Subject lines: specific, honest, curiosity with clarity; preview text; single CTA; mobile-first layout.
- Deliverability: authenticated domain (SPF/DKIM/DMARC), consent, easy unsubscribe, list hygiene.
- Compliance: CAN-SPAM, GDPR/ePrivacy, CASL as applicable.

### Social Media
- Choose 1-3 platforms by audience fit. Native formats; strong first line/frame; consistent cadence.
- Mix: value (educate/entertain), proof (customers/results), personality (behind the scenes), promotion (offers).
- Engage: reply, ask questions, collaborate; build community, not just broadcast.
- Influencer/creator partnerships: relevance and authenticity over follower count; disclose sponsorships per regulations.

### Paid Media (Search, Social, Display, Video, Retargeting)
- Start with clear conversion goals, tracking, and landing pages ready.
- Structure: campaign -> ad group/set -> creative; test creative, audiences, and offers systematically (one variable at a time).
- Search: intent-based keywords, negative keywords, ad copy matching query, landing page message match.
- Social/display: creative leads; hooks in the first 3 seconds; test formats.
- Optimize on cost per acquisition/ROAS and downstream quality (not just clicks). Watch frequency and fatigue.
- Retargeting with sensible caps and exclusions of converters.

### Partnerships, Events, PR, Community, Referral
- Co-marketing, affiliates, integrations; webinars and events with pre-/post-event nurture; PR with newsworthy angles and data; customer advocacy and referral loops.

### Product-Led & Lifecycle Growth
- Activation, onboarding, in-app messaging, free trial/freemium flows, expansion, retention, and reactivation.

## Step 4 - Copywriting & Creative
- **Frameworks:** AIDA (Attention, Interest, Desire, Action), PAS (Problem, Agitate, Solution), BAB (Before-After-Bridge), FAB (Feature-Advantage-Benefit).
- Lead with the benefit and outcome; use the customer's own words.
- Be specific: numbers, timeframes, names, concrete proof beat vague superlatives.
- One clear CTA with action verb and value ("Start your free trial," "Get the checklist").
- Social proof near the decision: testimonials (real, attributed, permissioned), reviews, logos, case-study results.
- Address objections (price, risk, effort, trust) with guarantees, FAQs, demos.
- Write short sentences; remove jargon; read aloud; match the brand voice.
- Visuals support the message, not decorate it; ensure accessibility (alt text, captions, contrast).
- Truthfulness: substantiate claims ("#1," "guaranteed," performance numbers); include required disclosures; avoid deceptive urgency, fake scarcity, or dark patterns.

## Step 5 - Conversion Optimization (CRO)
- Landing page: headline matches the ad/source (message match), clear value proposition, benefits, proof, one CTA, minimal navigation, fast load, mobile-friendly, short form with only needed fields.
- Funnel analysis: find the largest drop-off, hypothesize why, test fixes.
- Test properly: hypothesis, single main change, adequate sample and duration, pre-defined success metric.
- Use qualitative insight (session recordings, surveys, user tests) alongside analytics.

## Step 6 - Campaign Planning Template
1. **Objective & KPI** (e.g., 500 qualified demo requests in 8 weeks at <$120 CPL)
2. **Audience/segments** and key insight
3. **Offer & core message** (+ proof)
4. **Channels & tactics** with role of each in the funnel
5. **Creative & content list** (assets, formats, owners)
6. **Calendar** (pre-launch, launch, sustain, wrap) with dependencies
7. **Budget & forecast** (reach -> clicks -> leads -> opportunities -> revenue assumptions)
8. **Tracking plan** (UTMs, pixels, CRM fields, dashboards)
9. **Risks & contingencies**
10. **Post-campaign review**

## Measurement
- **Funnel metrics:** impressions, reach, CTR, CVR, CPL, CAC, MQL->SQL->Opportunity->Closed, ROAS, ROI, payback, LTV/CAC, retention, NPS.
- **Brand metrics:** share of voice, branded search, direct traffic, awareness and consideration surveys.
- **Attribution:** use multiple lenses (last-click, multi-touch, incrementality tests, self-reported attribution, MMM for mature budgets); recognize no model is perfect.
- Consistent UTM conventions; CRM-integrated reporting; separate leading indicators from lagging results.
- Report format: results vs goal, what worked, what didn't, why, and next actions.

## Brand Building
- Consistent identity (name, visuals, voice, story); distinctive assets (colors, shapes, sounds, characters of your own); reach broad audiences repeatedly.
- Purpose and values expressed through actions, not slogans.
- Avoid copying competitors' identities or using others' trademarks.

## B2B vs B2C Notes
- **B2B:** longer cycles, multiple stakeholders, account-based marketing, sales-marketing alignment, content for each role, trust and proof, lead scoring, pipeline focus.
- **B2C:** emotional triggers and speed, social proof, promotions, reviews, retention and loyalty loops, creative volume, mobile-first.

## Ethics, Privacy & Compliance
- Obtain consent for tracking and emails; honor opt-outs; minimize data collected; respect regional laws (GDPR, CCPA/CPRA, ePrivacy, CAN-SPAM, CASL).
- Advertising standards (FTC and local equivalents): truthful, substantiated, disclosed endorsements; special care for health, finance, children, and regulated products.
- Don't target using sensitive attributes where prohibited; avoid manipulative design.

## Common Mistakes
- Promoting before defining positioning and audience
- Features instead of benefits; generic claims
- Too many channels, no focus; inconsistent voice
- No tracking or goal before launch; vanity metrics
- Ignoring existing customers (retention is cheaper than acquisition)
- Landing page that doesn't match the ad
- Stopping tests too early; changing many variables at once
- Fake urgency, fake reviews, misleading claims

## Checklist
- [ ] Objective, KPI, and budget linked to business goals
- [ ] Audience insights and positioning documented
- [ ] Core message, offer, proof, and single CTA defined
- [ ] Channel mix justified; content and creative planned
- [ ] Tracking, UTMs, and CRM reporting set up before launch
- [ ] Landing page message-matched, fast, and mobile-ready
- [ ] Legal, privacy, and disclosure requirements checked
- [ ] Test plan and review cadence established
- [ ] Post-campaign learnings captured
