# Business Strategy Skill

> Strategy is a set of **choices**: where to play, how to win, and what *not* to do. A plan that tries to do everything is not a strategy.

## Core Principles
1. **Start with the decision.** What choice must leadership make, by when, with what resources?
2. **Facts before frameworks.** Frameworks organize thinking; they don't replace evidence. Look up current market data and cite it.
3. **Hypothesis-driven.** Form an early answer, then try to disprove it (issue tree -> hypotheses -> analyses).
4. **Customer-first.** Value is defined by the customer's problem and willingness to pay.
5. **Differentiation or cost advantage - and sustainability.** Why can you win, and why can't rivals copy it quickly?
6. **Trade-offs are explicit.** State what you will not do.
7. **Strategy is executable.** If it doesn't translate into priorities, owners, metrics, and budget, it's a wish.
8. **Be honest about uncertainty.** Use scenarios, assumptions, and leading indicators.

## Workflow
1. **Frame the problem** - objective, scope, constraints, time horizon, success metrics, decision-makers.
2. **Diagnose** - internal and external analysis.
3. **Generate options** - at least 3 distinct strategic alternatives (including "do less" and status quo).
4. **Evaluate** - criteria, economics, risks, fit with capabilities.
5. **Choose & focus** - recommendation with trade-offs.
6. **Plan execution** - initiatives, owners, milestones, KPIs, resources, governance.
7. **Monitor & adapt** - leading indicators and triggers for change.

## Step 1 - Frame the Problem
- Write a **problem statement**: "How should [company] [achieve objective] by [date] given [constraints]?"
- **Issue tree (MECE):** break the question into mutually exclusive, collectively exhaustive branches (e.g., Profit = Revenue - Cost; Revenue = Customers x Price x Frequency).
- List key hypotheses and the analysis that would prove or disprove each.
- Define "good": target growth, margin, share, ROI, payback, risk tolerance.

## Step 2 - Diagnose

### External
- **Market:** size, growth, segments, profit pools, trends, regulation, technology shifts (TAM/SAM/SOM with method; triangulate top-down and bottom-up).
- **Customers:** segments, jobs-to-be-done, needs, buying criteria, willingness to pay, switching costs, pain points.
- **Competitors:** players, share, positioning, pricing, business models, strengths/weaknesses, likely moves, new entrants, substitutes.
- **PESTEL:** Political, Economic, Social, Technological, Environmental, Legal forces.
- **Porter's Five Forces:** rivalry, threat of entrants, substitutes, buyer power, supplier power -> industry attractiveness.
- **Ecosystem/value chain:** where is value captured? Who holds leverage?

### Internal
- **Performance:** revenue, margin, growth, unit economics, retention, cash.
- **Capabilities & assets:** brand, technology, data, talent, distribution, cost position, partnerships.
- **VRIO:** is a resource Valuable, Rare, hard to Imitate, and Organized to capture value?
- **Core competencies** and constraints (capital, talent, culture, legacy systems).

### Synthesis
- **SWOT** only as a summary - turn it into choices: SO (use strengths to seize opportunities), WO, ST, WT strategies.
- Identify the **central strategic challenge** or insight ("the crux").

## Step 3 - Generate Strategic Options
Use lenses to broaden the option set:
- **Ansoff Matrix:** market penetration, market development, product development, diversification.
- **Generic strategies:** cost leadership, differentiation, focus.
- **Blue Ocean:** eliminate-reduce-raise-create to find uncontested space.
- **Business model levers:** who pays, what for, how (subscription, marketplace, licensing, freemium, usage-based, platform).
- **Build / Buy / Partner** for capabilities.
- **BCG matrix / portfolio view:** invest, hold, harvest, divest.
- **Three Horizons:** defend core (H1), build emerging (H2), explore options (H3).
- **Playing to Win cascade:** Winning aspiration -> Where to play -> How to win -> Capabilities required -> Management systems.

## Step 4 - Evaluate Options
Compare options on a transparent scorecard:
| Criteria | Questions |
|---|---|
| Attractiveness | Market size, growth, margins |
| Ability to win | Right to win, differentiation, capabilities |
| Economics | Investment, NPV/IRR, payback, unit economics, sensitivity |
| Risk | Execution, market, regulatory, technology, competitive response |
| Strategic fit | Brand, synergies, optionality |
| Speed & reversibility | Time to impact; can we stop or pivot cheaply? |
- Build a simple financial model with base/upside/downside cases.
- **Pre-mortem:** assume it failed in 2 years - why? Mitigate the top causes.
- **Game theory:** how will competitors respond? What if they cut price or copy?
- **What would you have to believe** for this option to be the best? Test those beliefs.

## Step 5 - Position & Differentiate
- **Value proposition:** For [target customer] who [need], [product] is a [category] that [key benefit], unlike [alternative], because [reason to believe].
- Segment -> Target -> Position (STP). Pick segments where you can be clearly better.
- Pricing strategy: value-based, competitive, cost-plus; test willingness to pay.
- Moats: network effects, switching costs, scale, brand, proprietary data/IP, regulatory, ecosystem.

## Step 6 - Execution Plan
- Translate into **3-5 strategic priorities** (not 15).
- **OKRs/KPIs** with baselines, targets, owners, and cadence; mix leading and lagging indicators.
- **Roadmap:** phases/quarters, dependencies, milestones, resource needs, budget.
- **Capabilities and org:** who must do what differently; hiring, training, partnerships.
- **Governance:** decision rights, review rhythm, escalation, stage gates.
- **Change management:** stakeholder map, communication plan, address resistance.
- **Risk register** with owners and mitigations; contingency triggers.
- **Quick wins** in the first 30-90 days to build momentum.

## Step 7 - Communicate
- **Answer first (Pyramid Principle):** recommendation -> 3 supporting reasons -> evidence.
- Executive summary (1 page): situation, insight, recommendation, financial impact, risks, ask/decision.
- Visuals: market map, 2x2 positioning, waterfall (profit bridge), roadmap, scenario table.
- Be explicit about assumptions, data sources/dates, and confidence level.

## Common Strategy Types - Quick Guides
- **Market entry:** attractiveness, entry mode (organic, partnership, acquisition), regulations, localization, go-to-market, break-even timeline.
- **Growth strategy:** growth vectors (new customers, ARPU, retention, new products/markets), sequencing, investment.
- **Turnaround:** stabilize cash, diagnose root causes, cut/fix/focus, rebuild, communicate.
- **Competitive response:** assess threat severity, customer impact, response options (ignore, match, differentiate, counterattack).
- **M&A / partnership:** strategic rationale, valuation range, synergies (revenue/cost, with realistic phasing), integration risk, alternatives.
- **Pricing strategy:** value drivers, segmentation, elasticity tests, packaging, discount governance.
- **Startup / new venture:** problem-solution fit, customer discovery, MVP, unit economics, funding runway, go-to-market.
- **Digital transformation:** customer journey, data and tech foundations, operating model, change capacity.

## Metrics to Know
Revenue growth, gross/contribution/operating margin, CAC, LTV, LTV/CAC, payback, churn/NRR, market share, ROIC, NPS, cash runway, capacity utilization, time-to-market.

## Common Mistakes
- Goals mistaken for strategy ("grow 20%") with no choices
- Framework tourism: filling in SWOT/Porter grids without insight
- Too many priorities; no explicit trade-offs
- Ignoring competitor reactions
- Optimistic top-down market sizing; hockey-stick forecasts
- No link to economics, owners, or metrics
- Analysis paralysis - or deciding without data
- Never revisiting assumptions

## Checklist
- [ ] Decision, objective, and success metrics defined
- [ ] Issue tree and hypotheses; evidence is current and sourced
- [ ] Market, customer, competitor, and internal diagnosis complete
- [ ] 3+ distinct options compared on explicit criteria
- [ ] Economics modeled with scenarios; risks and pre-mortem done
- [ ] Clear positioning and "what we will not do"
- [ ] Execution plan with owners, KPIs, milestones, budget
- [ ] Assumptions, uncertainties, and triggers for change documented
- [ ] Recommendation is answer-first and decision-ready
