# Content Creation Skill

## Core Principles
1. **Audience first.** Great content solves a real problem, answers a real question, or delivers a real feeling for a specific person.
2. **One idea per piece.** A clear single promise beats a scattered overview.
3. **Earn attention in the first line.** The hook decides whether the rest gets read.
4. **Value density.** Every paragraph should teach, prove, entertain, or move the reader forward; cut the rest.
5. **Specific beats generic.** Concrete examples, numbers, stories, and named details create credibility and memorability.
6. **Original perspective.** Add experience, data, opinion, or synthesis - don't just rewrite what already ranks.
7. **Distribution is half the job.** Plan how people will find it before you write it.
8. **Consistency compounds.** A sustainable cadence beats bursts.
9. **Truthful and original.** Verify facts, cite sources, credit others, never plagiarize or fabricate quotes, stats, or testimonials; disclose sponsorships and AI assistance where required.

## Step 1 - Strategy
- **Goal:** awareness, traffic, leads, trust/authority, community, sales enablement, retention.
- **Audience:** who they are, their problems, vocabulary, where they spend time, what content they already consume.
- **Positioning & voice:** your unique angle and 3-4 voice traits (e.g., practical, candid, curious) with do/don't examples.
- **Content pillars (3-5):** recurring themes tied to audience needs and business goals.
- **Formats & channels:** blog/SEO, newsletter, LinkedIn/X/Instagram/TikTok/YouTube, podcast, webinars; choose a few and do them well.
- **Content mix:** educate (60%), inspire/entertain (20%), prove/convert (20%) as a starting guideline.
- **Success metrics:** match to goal (e.g., subscribers, qualified leads, saves/shares, watch time, branded search).

## Step 2 - Research & Idea Generation
- Mine: customer calls/support tickets/sales objections, community questions, search queries and "People also ask," comments, competitor gaps, trending topics, internal expertise, data you own.
- Validate with demand signals (search volume, repeated questions, engagement on similar posts).
- Idea formulas: How-to, Mistakes to avoid, Myths vs facts, Case study/teardown, Checklist/template, Comparison, Behind-the-scenes, Contrarian take, Predictions/trends, Interview/expert roundup, Original data, Story of a failure/lesson.
- Keep an **idea bank** with angle, audience, format, and priority.

## Step 3 - The Content Brief
Before drafting, define:
- **Working title & primary promise** ("After reading, they will be able to...")
- **Audience & stage** (awareness/consideration/decision)
- **Search intent / key question(s)**
- **Core argument or key takeaways (3-5)**
- **Outline & evidence needed** (examples, data, quotes, visuals)
- **CTA** (one primary action)
- **Format/length, tone, deadline, sources to use**

## Step 4 - Structure & Storytelling
### Hooks (first 1-2 lines)
- Bold claim, surprising stat, question, relatable pain, mini-story, counterintuitive insight, clear promise ("In 10 minutes you'll...").
- Test several; the best hook is specific and creates curiosity or recognition.

### Frameworks
- **AIDA:** Attention -> Interest -> Desire -> Action.
- **PAS:** Problem -> Agitate -> Solution.
- **Inverted pyramid:** key info first (news, updates).
- **Story arc:** Setup -> Conflict -> Struggle -> Resolution -> Lesson.
- **Listicle:** numbered items each with a heading, explanation, example.
- **Skyscraper / depth:** cover the topic more completely and clearly than existing top results.
- **Before-After-Bridge.**

### Article Skeleton
1. Title (clear + compelling) 2. Hook 3. Context/why it matters 4. Preview of what's covered (optional) 5. Body sections with descriptive subheads 6. Examples/evidence 7. Summary/key takeaways 8. CTA.

### Storytelling Techniques
- Make a specific person the protagonist (customer, you, a team); show stakes, a turning point, and measurable outcome.
- "Show, don't tell": scenes, dialogue snippets, sensory details where appropriate.
- Use analogies to explain complexity; use contrast and tension to maintain interest.

## Step 5 - Writing Craft
- Write for one reader; conversational but precise; active voice; short sentences with varied rhythm; concrete nouns and strong verbs.
- Lead paragraphs with the point; one idea per paragraph; use subheads, bullets, bold sparingly, and whitespace for scanning.
- Replace vague claims with evidence: numbers, names, examples, quotes.
- Cut filler ("very," "just," "in order to," throat-clearing intros); delete the first paragraph if it only warms up.
- Reading level appropriate to the audience (often grade 7-9 for general audiences).
- Transitions that pull readers forward; open loops carefully; end sections with momentum.
- Avoid clickbait that the content doesn't pay off; trust is the asset.
- Headlines: specific benefit, number or timeframe when honest, clear keyword when relevant, ~50-65 characters for search; write 5-10 options and choose the clearest.

## Step 6 - Format-Specific Guidance

### Blog / SEO Articles
Match search intent; use a clear H1, logical H2/H3, answer the main question early, original insights, internal links, descriptive meta title/description, images with alt text, updated date, author credentials. Refresh older posts regularly.

### Newsletters
Strong subject line + preview text; one main idea; personal voice; skimmable structure; one primary CTA; consistent send time; give value even if no one clicks; respect consent and unsubscribe rules.

### Social Posts
- **Platform-native:** short vertical video (TikTok/Reels/Shorts), carousels (Instagram/LinkedIn), threads and short text (X, Threads, LinkedIn), community discussion (Reddit/Facebook groups).
- First line/first 2 seconds must stop the scroll; one point per post; ask a question or invite replies; avoid hashtag stuffing; reply to comments quickly.
- Repurpose a core idea into multiple native formats rather than cross-posting identically.

### Video & Podcast Scripts
Hook (0-5 s) -> promise -> content in tight beats -> pattern interrupts (visuals, cuts, examples) -> payoff -> CTA. Write for the ear: short sentences, contractions, spoken rhythm. Add timestamps, chapters, titles, thumbnails/cover concepts, descriptions, captions, and transcripts.

### Case Studies
Customer context -> challenge -> solution -> implementation -> measurable results (before/after) -> customer quote (approved) -> lessons/next steps. Get permission and verify every number.

### Long-Form (guides, ebooks, whitepapers, reports)
Executive summary, clear chapter structure, original data/frameworks, visuals, downloadable checklist/templates, gated vs ungated decision based on goal.

### Landing & Sales Pages
Benefit-led headline, subhead, proof, how it works, objections/FAQ, single CTA; see the marketing skill for conversion detail.

## Step 7 - Visuals & Media
- Purposeful images, diagrams, charts, screenshots, and thumbnails; consistent brand style; compress for speed; add alt text/captions; avoid unlicensed images; no misleading edits.
- Data visuals: one message per chart; label clearly; cite source.
- Accessibility: captions, transcripts, sufficient contrast, descriptive link text.

## Step 8 - Edit & Quality Control (multiple passes)
1. **Structure:** does it deliver the promise? logical order? missing or redundant sections?
2. **Substance:** facts verified; claims sourced; examples relevant; originality added.
3. **Clarity & concision:** cut 10-20%; simplify sentences; define jargon.
4. **Voice & tone:** consistent with brand; human, not robotic.
5. **Line edit & proofread:** grammar, spelling, names, numbers, links, formatting; read aloud.
6. **Compliance:** attribution, permissions, disclosures (ads, affiliate links, sponsorships), claims substantiation.
- If AI tools assist drafting: treat output as a first draft; fact-check everything; add real experience and examples; remove generic filler; keep a human accountable for accuracy and voice.

## Step 9 - Distribution & Promotion
- Publish where the audience is: owned (site, email, community), earned (PR, guest posts, partnerships, mentions), paid (boost proven content), shared (employees, customers, creators).
- Promotion checklist: email list, 3-5 social variations over time, relevant communities (follow rules), internal sales/support teams, partners, republishing/syndication with canonical tags, outreach to people quoted or cited.
- Make it easy to share: quotable lines, tweetable stats, visuals.

## Step 10 - Repurpose & Refresh
- One flagship asset -> many pieces: article -> newsletter issue, carousel, short clips, quote graphics, podcast episode, webinar, checklist, email series, FAQ entries.
- Update top performers every 6-12 months (new data, examples, links); consolidate thin overlapping posts.

## Step 11 - Measure & Improve
- **Leading:** impressions, CTR, watch time/retention, scroll depth, saves, shares, replies.
- **Lagging:** subscribers, leads, pipeline, revenue influenced, rankings, backlinks.
- Review monthly: what topics, formats, hooks, and CTAs perform? Double down on winners; retire losers; test one variable at a time.
- Qualitative: comments, replies, sales feedback.

## Content Calendar & Workflow
- Plan 4-12 weeks ahead: date, channel, pillar, title, owner, status (idea -> brief -> draft -> edit -> design -> approved -> scheduled -> published -> promoted -> reviewed).
- Batch similar tasks; keep a buffer of ready content; define roles and approval steps; maintain templates and a style guide (voice, formatting, terminology, citation rules).

## Ethics & Legal
- Original work or properly licensed material; cite and link sources; no fake reviews, testimonials, or invented quotes; don't make unverified health/financial/legal claims; disclose affiliate/sponsored content; respect privacy and consent; avoid impersonating real people or organizations; avoid copying others' characters, logos, or protected works.

## Common Mistakes
- Writing for "everyone"; no clear promise or CTA
- Weak or misleading headlines/hooks
- Rehashing top-ranking content without new value
- Posting inconsistently or quitting before compounding starts
- Publishing without distribution plans
- Ignoring data; chasing vanity metrics
- Over-polished corporate tone with no personality
- Cross-posting identical content to every platform
- Skipping fact-checking and proofreading

## Checklist
- [ ] Goal, audience, pillar, and primary promise defined
- [ ] Brief with outline, evidence, CTA, and intent
- [ ] Strong hook and clear structure; one idea per piece
- [ ] Specific, original, sourced, and fact-checked
- [ ] Edited for clarity, voice, and accuracy; proofread
- [ ] Visuals/accessibility done; legal and disclosure checks passed
- [ ] Distribution and repurposing plan ready
- [ ] Metrics tracked; refresh/iteration scheduled
