# Graphic Design Skill

## Core Principles
1. **Design solves a communication problem.** Define purpose, audience, and the single message before choosing any visual.
2. **Hierarchy first.** The viewer should know what to look at first, second, and third within seconds.
3. **Simplicity wins.** Remove everything that doesn't support the message. White space is a design element.
4. **Consistency builds trust.** Repeat type, color, spacing, and style rules.
5. **Contrast creates clarity.** Size, weight, color, and space differences direct attention.
6. **Constraints fuel creativity.** Work within brand, format, and budget limits deliberately.
7. **Originality over imitation.** Draw on principles and references for inspiration, but don't copy protected artworks, logos, or characters.

## Step 1 - The Brief
Capture before designing:
- **Goal:** inform, sell, persuade, announce, entertain? Desired action (call, visit, buy, share)?
- **Audience:** who, age, context, culture, accessibility needs.
- **Message:** one sentence; supporting points ranked.
- **Content:** exact text, logos, images, data, legal copy, contact details, QR/URL.
- **Format & channel:** size, orientation, print vs screen, viewing distance, file type.
- **Brand:** colors, fonts, logo rules, tone, existing templates.
- **Constraints:** budget, deadline, print method, accessibility, language(s).
- **Success criteria:** how will we know it works?
If critical info is missing, state your assumptions and proceed.

## Step 2 - Concept & Layout
- Sketch 3+ quick thumbnails exploring different compositions before committing.
- Choose a **big idea**: a visual metaphor, a dominant image, a bold type treatment, or a data-led graphic.
- Pick a layout structure: grid (columns/rows), modular, centered/symmetrical, asymmetrical, full-bleed image, split screen.
- Use a **grid** and consistent margins/gutters. Align elements to it.

## Design Principles in Practice

### Hierarchy & Focal Point
- Establish one dominant element (headline or image). Support with 2-3 clear levels.
- Use scale, weight, color, position, and isolation to rank information.
- Reading patterns: Z-pattern (simple layouts), F-pattern (text-heavy), top-left start in LTR languages.

### Alignment, Proximity, Repetition, Contrast (CRAP)
- **Alignment:** every element lines up with something; choose left, center, or right and stay consistent.
- **Proximity:** group related items; separate unrelated ones.
- **Repetition:** reuse styles, shapes, spacing for cohesion.
- **Contrast:** make differences obvious, not timid.

### White Space (Negative Space)
- Generous margins and breathing room increase perceived quality and legibility.
- Don't fill every gap; use space to isolate the key message.

### Balance & Rhythm
- Symmetrical (formal, stable) vs asymmetrical (dynamic, modern).
- Use consistent spacing units (e.g., multiples of 8 px or a baseline grid).

### Imagery
- High resolution (300 dpi for print at final size; 2x for retina screens), well lit, relevant, consistent style.
- Crop deliberately; mind the rule of thirds and eye lines; leave space for text.
- Overlay text on images only with enough contrast (gradient, scrim, or solid box).
- Use licensed or original assets; check usage rights; avoid copying copyrighted work.
- Photography > clip art; illustration styles should be consistent across a set.

## Typography
- **Limit to 2 typefaces** (often one serif + one sans, or a single family with multiple weights).
- **Pairing:** contrast in structure but harmony in proportions; avoid two similar-but-different fonts.
- **Hierarchy via scale:** use a type scale (e.g., 1.25x or 1.5x steps): display, heading, subheading, body, caption.
- **Body legibility:** 9-12 pt print; 16 px+ on screens; line length 45-75 characters; line height 1.4-1.6x; adequate letter spacing.
- **Alignment:** left-aligned body text for readability; avoid justified text without hyphenation; center only for short lines.
- Avoid: all-caps paragraphs, tiny light gray text, stretched or distorted fonts, too many weights, widows/orphans, rivers.
- Check fonts' licenses and availability; embed or outline fonts for print files.
- Kerning and tracking: adjust headlines optically; add tracking to small caps and all-caps text.

## Color
- **Palette:** 1 dominant + 1-2 accents + neutrals. Follow the 60-30-10 rule as a starting point.
- **Meaning & culture:** colors carry different associations across cultures and industries; choose intentionally.
- **Harmony:** monochromatic, analogous, complementary, triadic, split-complementary.
- **Contrast & accessibility:** WCAG AA = 4.5:1 for body text, 3:1 for large text; don't rely on color alone to convey meaning; test for color-blindness.
- **Color modes:** RGB/HEX for screen; CMYK or Pantone (spot) for print; convert and proof - bright RGB colors (neon) can't be reproduced in CMYK.
- Use consistent tints and shades from the palette rather than new colors.

## Logo & Brand Identity
- A good logo is **simple, memorable, versatile, timeless, and appropriate**.
- Design in vector; test in black & white, small sizes, on light and dark backgrounds, and as a favicon/app icon.
- Provide variants: primary, horizontal, stacked, icon-only, mono, reversed. Specify clear space and minimum size.
- Brand guidelines: logo usage, colors (HEX/RGB/CMYK/Pantone), typography, imagery style, voice, do/don't examples.
- Don't imitate existing marks; research the market to avoid conflicts. Original concepts only.

## Format-Specific Guidance
- **Poster/Flyer:** one headline readable from a distance, one image, essential details (who/what/when/where), clear call to action, contact/QR. Few words.
- **Social graphics:** safe zones for platform cropping, large legible text on mobile, strong first-frame/thumbnail, consistent templates, platform-correct sizes.
- **Infographic:** one story, logical flow, simple icons, consistent chart styles, data labeled and sourced, accurate scale.
- **Presentation slides:** large type, one idea per slide, high-contrast, consistent master.
- **Packaging:** dieline accuracy, legal text, barcodes, shelf impact, material/finish considerations.
- **Print collateral (brochure, business card):** bleed (typically 3 mm / 0.125 in), safe margin (3-5 mm), fold marks, 300 dpi, CMYK, PDF/X export.
- **Web/UI graphics:** responsive sizes, SVG for icons/logos, optimized file weights (WebP/AVIF/PNG/JPG), alt text.

## Production & Delivery
- **Print:** CMYK, 300 dpi, 3 mm bleed, crop marks as requested, fonts outlined or embedded, links packaged, rich black guidance, proof before printing, confirm paper/finish.
- **Screen:** sRGB, correct pixel dimensions, compression balanced for quality, accessible alt text.
- **File management:** layered master file + exported deliverables; clear names (`Brand_Poster_A3_v02.pdf`); keep source fonts/assets.
- **Spec check:** text spell-checked and verified against provided copy; contact data and legal copy correct; QR code tested.

## Critique Framework
Evaluate in this order:
1. **Purpose:** is the message clear in 3 seconds?
2. **Hierarchy:** is the eye led correctly?
3. **Layout:** alignment, spacing, balance, grid.
4. **Typography:** legibility, pairing, scale.
5. **Color:** palette, contrast, mood, accessibility.
6. **Imagery:** quality, relevance, consistency.
7. **Brand fit & originality.**
8. **Technical:** resolution, bleed, color mode, file format.
Give feedback as: *What works -> What to improve -> Specific suggestion*. Squint test and thumbnail test: blur or shrink the design - does the hierarchy still read?

## Accessibility
- Sufficient contrast; minimum font sizes; avoid text in images when possible (or provide alt text).
- Don't depend on color alone; add shapes, labels, or patterns.
- Avoid flashing/animation that can trigger discomfort; provide captions for video.
- Consider dyslexia-friendly choices: clear sans-serif, generous spacing, left alignment.

## Ethics & Legal
- Respect copyright, trademarks, and image licenses; don't reproduce existing logos, artwork, or characters.
- Represent people inclusively and truthfully; avoid stereotypes and misleading imagery.
- Disclose edited/AI-generated imagery where required; obtain model releases where applicable.
- No deceptive designs (fake endorsements, impersonating real organizations).

## Common Mistakes
- No clear focal point; everything shouted at once
- Too many fonts, colors, effects
- Low-contrast text over busy images
- Inconsistent margins and alignment
- Low-resolution or stretched images
- Text too small or lines too long
- Ignoring print specs (bleed, color mode)
- Decoration over communication

## Checklist
- [ ] Goal, audience, message, and call to action defined
- [ ] Clear hierarchy and focal point (passes the squint test)
- [ ] Grid, alignment, and spacing consistent
- [ ] Max 2 typefaces; legible sizes; line lengths comfortable
- [ ] Limited palette; contrast meets accessibility
- [ ] Images high-quality, licensed, and relevant
- [ ] Brand rules followed (or created and documented)
- [ ] Proofread; facts, links, and QR codes verified
- [ ] Correct file specs and export formats delivered
